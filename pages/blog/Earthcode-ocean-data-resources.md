# Dive Into Open Science: Exploring EarthCODE’s Ocean Data Resources <!--{ as="img" mode="hero" src="https://esa-earthcode.github.io/portal-assets/blog/esa-mediterranean-datacube.png?raw=true" }-->

## Dive Into Open Science: Exploring EarthCODE’s Ocean Data Resources
*Published: 24.09.2026*

EarthCODE is the environment that brings ESA’s vision of a culture and practice of openness in Earth Observation (EO) science, applications and industry to life. It provides a sustainable, open innovation ecosystem, with FAIR principles (Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability), Open Science, and Open Innovation.

EarthCODE is a community-driven initiative that works closely with the ESA Science Clusters. Together, they create opportunities for scalable, cross-sector research and decision-making.

## What is the EarthCODE Open Science Catalogue?
The [Open Science Catalogue](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/) is a one stop-platform for discovering and accessing publicly available geoscience products, datasets, and resources from scientific research projects funded by ESA EO. Whether you’re studying climate change, marine biodiversity, or natural disasters, the catalogue provides the metadata and direct links to the data you need. Browse the [full catalogue here](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/catalog/).

## What Ocean Data are Available?
A wealth of ocean data is made available within EarthCODE’s Open Science Catalogue. For a complete list of ocean datasets, visit the Open Science Catalogue [Oceans theme webpage](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/themes/oceans/catalog). These datasets are critical for understanding ocean dynamics, climate change impacts, and marine biodiversity.

EarthCODE has also curated a selection of ocean datasets to support research and innovation in ocean science. To support innovation in ocean science, EarthCODE has curated a selection of datasets for the upcoming ESA Ocean Science Cluster Hackathon (October 2026). These datasets are not only freely accessible but also come with pre-built notebooks to help users get started quickly. To access the notebooks for these datasets, visit the [Ocean Hackathon dataset webpage](https://esa-earthcode.github.io/ocean_hackathon/datasets-sumary/).

## What Other Resources are Available?
Beyond datasets, the Open Science Catalogue also offers powerful tools for exploration and analysis:
- Search by [EO Mission](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/eo-missions/catalog), [Experiment](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/experiments/catalog), [Product](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/products/catalog), [Project](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/projects/catalog), [Theme](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/themes/catalog), and [Variable](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/variables/catalog): Find exactly what you need with customisable filters
- [Workflow Catalogue](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/workflows/catalog): Access pre-built workflows to streamline your research
- [Integrated EO Platforms](https://earthcode.esa.int/ecosystem): Develop and run experiments using EarthCODE’s cloud-based computation resources

In addition to the Open Science Catalogue, EarthCODE has also developed two specialised Datacubes:
- ESA Mediterranean Datacube: a combined dataset of indicators and variables for the Mediterranean Sea, including temperature, salinity, ocean currents, and more, see Figure 2. Explore the [datacube here](https://earthcode.esa.int/data-collections/mediterranean-cube).
- ESA Antarctica Datacube: a comprehensive collection of Antarctic variables. See [this blog](https://earthcode.esa.int/blog/polar-hackathon-polar-data) to learn more.

The Mediterranean Datacube is stored in GeoZarr, a cloud-optimised format that enables fast, scalable processing. Providing data from 2016 to 2022, it uses a standardised, time-series enabled grid to foster reproducible and collaborative science. [Lampata](https://lampata.co.uk/), an EarthCODE partner, has created a series of interactive notebooks to help users explore the Mediterranean Datacube. Access the [notebooks here](https://esa-earthcode.github.io/ocean_hackathon/intro/).

## How to get Started
To get started with the EarthCODE Ocean Data, follow these steps:
1.  Explore the Open Science Catalogue: Visit the EarthCODE [Open Science Catalogue](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/) to browse datasets by theme or project.
2.  Access the Hackathon Notebooks: Access pre-built notebooks for the featured ocean datasets listed in [the table here](https://esa-earthcode.github.io/ocean_hackathon/datasets-sumary/).
3.  Dive into the Mediterranean Datacube: Learn how to use the Mediterranean Datacube with [Lampata’s notebooks](https://esa-earthcode.github.io/ocean_hackathon/remote-cube-access/).
4.  Create using integrated EO Platforms: Access data, develop workflows and run experiments using [EarthCODE's accessible computation resources](https://earthcode.esa.int/ecosystem).
5.  Join the Community: Engage with the EarthCODE community via the [EarthCODE Forum](https://discourse-earthcode.eox.at/) to collaborate with other researchers and contribute to open science.
