# Polar Hackathon Success! <!--{ as="img" mode="hero" src="https://earthcode.esa.int/img/Antarctica_In_Sync_visualisation.jpeg?raw=true" }-->
## Polar Hackathon Success!
*Published: 29.07.2026*

The **EarthCODE Polar Hackathon**, held online in the last week of June 2026, brought together 15 participants from the **ESA Polar Science Cluster** community. Led by EarthCODE's [Lampata](https://lampata.co.uk/), the event introduced attendees to a repository of notebooks designed to help users:
- Discover Antarctic datasets,
- Convert them into cloud-native formats,
- Combine them into analysis-ready collections, and
- Integrate these collections into scientific workflows.

All the polar datasets are open and accessible via the EarthCODE [Open Science Catalog](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/), with each dataset paired with a guidance notebook to support analysis. To further streamline access to scientific Antarctic data, selected datasets were consolidated into an Antarctic data cube.

IMAGE

## Hackathon Outcomes
The event confirmed strong interest in building a community around ESA data cubes and related datasets, particularly within the Polar Science Cluster. Key takeaways included:
- Value in the cube-based approach: Participants saw it as a repeatable model for future annual or recurring activities.
- Dataset preparation: Continued efforts are needed, including:
  - Adding a sea ice dataset,
  - Maintaining the existing Antarctic data cube materials.
- Future expansion: Interest in exploring an Arctic data cube as a next step.
- Materials as a reference: The hackathon’s notebooks, dataset descriptions, and follow-up resources should serve as the entry point for future Polar Cluster work.

Participants actively used the [EarthCODE Discourse Forum](https://earthcode.esa.int/community/) to ask questions, suggest improvements, and troubleshoot technical issues. This allowed the EarthCODE team to provide timely support and foster collaboration.

IMAGE

## Positive Feedback
Attendees highly valued:
- The Antarctic data cube,
- Example notebooks,
- The opportunity to work directly with ESA EarthCODE datasets.

The Polar Cluster Lead noted that the data cube and notebooks would be promoted at the upcoming Polar Cluster co-location meeting and the Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR) Open Science Conference in August.

## Lessons for the EarthCODE Hackathon
The EarthCODE Hackathon 2026 will be an in-person event in ESA ESRIN, bringing together researchers, data scientists, and open-data enthusiasts. Lessons from the Polar Hackathon will shape its structure:
1. More time for familiarisation:
   - The Polar Hackathon ran over three afternoons in a single week, but participants needed more time to explore the data, tools, and workflows.
   - The EarthCODE Hackathon 2026 (30 November – 4 December 2026) will run for four full days, from Monday to Friday, providing ample opportunity to dive deeper and produce meaningful outputs.
2. Dedicated orientation session:
   - The first day will include a structured familiarisation session covering:
     - EarthCODE platform,
     - Workspace navigation,
     - The data cube and its datasets,
     - Expected outputs.
   - A clearer distinction will be made between the data cube itself and the underlying datasets.
3. Pre-hackathon engagement:
   - Pre-event meetings will be organised to support setup activities, allowing participants to focus on science and collaboration during the main event.

## Looking Ahead
For the polar community, this hackathon laid a strong foundation ahead of the main event later this year. To ensure its success, similar online hackathons are planned for the ESA Ocean and Carbon Science Clusters.
