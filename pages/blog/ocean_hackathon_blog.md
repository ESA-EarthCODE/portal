## Productive Ocean Hackathon
*Published: 18.11.2026*

The EarthCODE Ocean Hackathon, held over three online sessions in late October 2026, brought together 20 participants from the ESA Ocean Science Cluster community. Led by Lampata, the event focused on exploring the Mediterranean Sea data cube. A data cube is a structured way to organise and analyse large datasets, making it easier to explore variables for the Mediterranean Sea. The online event delivered several key outcomes:
* **Shared dataset access:** Researchers explored prepared ESA ocean datasets in compatible formats, with Jupyter notebooks for direct cloud access
* **Hands-on science practice:** Teams applied ocean data cubes to real questions and presented exploratory maps, indicators, and methods
* **Community knowledge exchange:** Participants shared scientific approaches with each other, and public tutorials and notebooks support reuse

## Ocean Hackathon Success
During the online hackathon participants were introduced to EarthCODE tools, shared computing environments, and example notebooks before deciding on scientific questions to investigate using the available datasets. For more details on the Ocean datasets available from EarthCODE, visit the [Open Science Catalogue](https://opensciencedata.esa.int/) or check out our recent blog post: [Dive Into Open Science: Exploring EarthCODE’s Ocean Data Resources](https://earthcode.esa.int/blog/Earthcode-ocean-data-resources). All Ocean Hackathon materials, including datasets, tutorials, and notebooks, are freely available on the [hackathon webpage](https://esa-earthcode.github.io/ocean_hackathon/). 

After the introductions, the participants then had time to explore the curated ocean datasets and test analysis workflows before presenting early results to peers and ESA scientists and discussing potential applications in their ongoing research. These preliminary scientific analyses included work on marine heatwaves, ocean fronts, vertical ocean dynamics, and coastal ecosystems.

## Feedback Informing the Future
Positive feedback was received; participants were particularly appreciative of the quality, accessibility, and organisation of the Mediterranean Sea data cube and the technical support provided. Participants highlighted the value of accessing multiple datasets through a common interface, enabling faster scientific analysis and exploration of variables outside their usual research domains. Several expressed interest in continuing to use the data in ongoing research, including studies of marine ecosystems, climate pressures and coastal processes.

Participants also shared valuable feedback for improvement, including additional datasets to be added to the data cube. This feedback will inform further development of the Mediterranean Sea data cube and preparations for the in-person [EarthCODE Hackathon](https://earthcode.esa.int/blog/Hackathon-2026), being held at ESA ESRIN, Italy, on 30 November – 4 December 2026.

## Get Involved!
Interested in ocean science? The Ocean Hackathon materials, including open datasets, tutorials, and notebooks, are freely available to everyone. These resources offer a unique opportunity to:
* **Explore** cutting-edge ocean science data,
* **Work** with reproducible tools and workflows,
* **Engage** with a community-driven approach.
By providing open access to ocean and Mediterranean Sea data and tools, EarthCODE supports sustainable research practices, enabling scientists worldwide to collaborate on critical ocean health challenges. Therefore, whether you’re a researcher, student, or data enthusiast, these materials are your gateway to discovering the Mediterranean Sea and the wider oceans including temperature, sea surface salinity, Chlorophyll-a, and more.

For more information, check out our [blog on the Ocean data](https://earthcode.esa.int/blog/Earthcode-ocean-data-resources) and the [online Ocean Hackathon webpage](https://esa-earthcode.github.io/ocean_hackathon/).
