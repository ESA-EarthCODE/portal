## Open Science Persistent Demonstrator
*Published: 13.04.2026*

The Open Science Persistent Demonstrator (OSPD) project is a collaborative initiative led by Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC). It was designed to advance open, standards-based, reproducible, and interoperable scientific research, especially in Earth Observation (EO) and related Earth sciences. The OSPD is an inter-agency pilot that treats workflow reuse as an interoperability problem; it aims to make scientific methods portable across heterogeneous EO infrastructures by capturing the workflow, its inputs, and its execution assumptions in standards-conformant, machine-readable form, so that results can be reproduced and adapted without re-engineering the method for each platform.

This project has been sponsored by the European Space Agency (ESA) and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), and co-funded by the European Union and the Institute for Geospatial Understanding through an Integrative Discovery Environment (I-GUIDE). The participants of the project explored Ontologies and OGC Building Blocks for reusing workflows across various platforms, as well as to develop research studies. The participants included CS Group (France), EOX, Geolabs, Hartis, I-GUIDE, Terradue, Lampata, and Telespazio.

The second pilot of the OSPD was supported by ESA’s EarthCODE initiative, of which there were four cross-over participants (Telespazio, EOX, Terradue, and Lampata). Early outcomes from OSPD provided valuable inputs for EarthCODE and feedback on the current approach to metadata handling for improved interoperability. EarthCODE supported the OSPD Pilot 2 by providing concrete requirements, including on aspects related to federation across national and European infrastructures.

In parallel, the OSPD has consolidated an initial set of Open Science workflow building blocks in the [OGC incubator](https://github.com/ogcincubator/bblocks-openscience) - modular specification artefacts that document how common workflow concepts (packaging, parameters, platforms, and execution records) can be represented and composed using existing OGC standards and reusable schema/ontology components.

As part of the Pilot, FAIR workflow principles were implemented through OGC standards and building blocks - particularly by aligning workflow execution to standards such as OGC API - Processes and by making workflow description explicit (inputs, parameters, execution environment, and provenance).

Through involvement in initiatives such as the OSPD, EarthCODE supports efforts for the standardisation of FAIR workflows and experiments - including the execution context required for re-use and their description in the [Open Science Catalogue (OSC)](https://opensciencedata.esa.int). The initiative has provided a practical testbed where workflow portability is validated against real platform heterogeneity rather than assumed in abstract.

Results of the OSPD Pilot 2 are openly available in the [Engineering Report](http://ospd-2025-2cf271.pages.ogc.org/).
